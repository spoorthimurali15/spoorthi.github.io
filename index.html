I am a computer science and engineering graduate currently working as an Advanced Application Engineering Analyst at Accenture. My primary research interests lie in computer vision, medical image analysis, and deep learning. My recent work includes developing an anatomy-regularized TensoRF framework for 3D lung nodule volumetry from sparse X-ray projections — integrating NeRF-based reconstruction, Beer–Lambert physics-based DRR simulation, and anatomy-aware regularization to achieve clinically actionable pulmonary nodule estimation without CT-level radiation exposure. I have also developed AMN, a hybrid dual-encoder architecture combining Swin Transformer and ResNet-50 for multi-class nuclei segmentation in histopathology, incorporating adaptive multi-scale feature fusion, boundary-aware learning, and uncertainty estimation for robust cross-domain generalization. I am deeply passionate about advancing AI for healthcare and am actively seeking PhD opportunities to pursue rigorous, long-term research in medical imaging and computational pathology.

Anatomy-Regularized TensoRF for Sparse-View Lung Nodule Volumetry from Digitally Reconstructed Radiographs † Under Review
Feb 2026 – May 2026
Amrita School of Engineering, Bangalore
Developed an advanced deep learning framework for 3D lung nodule reconstruction and volumetric analysis from only three orthogonal X-ray projections. Leveraged Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and TensoRF architectures to address sparse-view medical imaging challenges while minimizing radiation exposure from conventional CT scans. Implemented anatomy-aware regularization combining sparsity constraints and total variation smoothness. Integrated Beer–Lambert physics-based DRR simulation and CT preprocessing pipelines using the LIDC-IDRI dataset.
Identified and resolved a critical density parameterization issue in TensoRF for medical attenuation fields, significantly improving gradient flow and reconstruction stability. Achieved high volumetric correlation with radiologist-annotated ground truth.

AMN: Adaptive Multi-Scale Fusion Network with Boundary and Uncertainty Modeling for Nuclei Segmentation † Under Review
Feb 2026 – Apr 2026
Amrita School of Engineering, Bangalore
Developed a hybrid deep learning framework for multi-class nuclei segmentation in histopathology images using a dual-encoder architecture combining Swin Transformer and ResNet-50. Implemented an adaptive multi-scale fusion mechanism with channel-wise gating to dynamically combine transformer and CNN features. Designed a multi-task prediction framework incorporating segmentation, boundary detection, classification, and uncertainty estimation. Evaluated on CoNIC and MoNuSeg datasets with strong cross-domain generalization without retraining.
Introduced uncertainty-aware loss functions and boundary-aware learning strategies. Achieved robust performance across multiple nuclei classes in densely packed, class-imbalanced histopathology images.
